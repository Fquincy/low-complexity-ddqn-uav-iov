MAIN FINDING
The completed run exhibits a shared positive-Q bound violation. In this
finite-horizon environment every immediate reward is nonpositive: delay
accrual, UAV energy, and deadline-miss terms are nonnegative and the reward
subtracts each term (Source/chapter3_iov_env_v2.py, reward calculation).
Episodes terminate after 40 slots, and successful horizon termination skips
the bootstrap target. Therefore every true finite-horizon action value is
nonpositive. A learned network may predict otherwise, but such a positive
value cannot be the return of any physically feasible continuation.

The read-only calculation reloaded the nine frozen final checkpoints and ran
them on the final saved replay states and the fixed common heuristic-probe
states. Across the three EMC models, 51.4%, 51.4%, and 58.3% of replay states
had positive greedy Q; all 128 common probe states had positive greedy Q for
each of the three EMC models. The additive64 and additive128 models showed the
same pattern: 42.8%-58.6% positive greedy Q on replay states and 100% on the
common probe states. All saved replay rewards and realized episode return-to-go
values were nonpositive. This identifies a shared learned-value error; the
probe-state result may also reflect distribution shift because probes were
generated along heuristic trajectories.

POLICY LEARNING VERSUS VALUE LEARNING
The policies did learn useful behavior during training. Across all nine runs,
mean training-episode return improved from roughly -722 to -742 over episodes
1-50 to roughly -58 to -81 over episodes151-200. Scheduled validation returns
typically improved from roughly -267 to -458 at episode0 to roughly -20 to -64
at episode200, although individual runs varied. EMC's three fresh-gate mean
returns were -66.82, -39.75, and -37.85; the baselines and heuristic policies
still performed better under the frozen screens.

The values moved in the opposite direction from reliable calibration. For EMC,
mean terminal Q in scheduled validation at episode200 was +20.52, +35.77, and
+41.80 for model seeds2309,2333,2351. The p95 absolute error between terminal
Q and the realized final-slot reward was 47.85,40.23,55.85, against the
predeclared limit10. Additive models showed the same upward terminal-Q trend;
their episode200 terminal-Q means ranged from +13.74 to +57.28 across runs.

The final replay Monte Carlo return-to-go averages around -228 to -251 by
model, while final replay greedy-Q means range around -81 to -120. This gap is
only diagnostic: replay follow-up actions were generated by an epsilon-greedy
behavior policy and are not an optimal continuation. The stronger result is
the sign-bound violation above, which does not depend on treating exploratory
Monte Carlo returns as optimal values.

OPTIMIZER EVIDENCE
For every architecture and initialization, the mean Huber loss rose after the
first quarter of updates. First-quarter means were about4.4-4.7; later-quarter
means were about9.1-12.4. Gradient norms exceeded the clip threshold10 in
about88%-93% of the first-quarter updates and in100% of updates in each later
quarter. Clipping is functioning as configured, but nearly universal clipping
means the optimizer is constrained by the chosen scale and trajectory. This
is correlated evidence, not proof that clipping caused the value error.

EXPLANATION BOUNDARY
The saved evidence supports a shared bootstrapped value instability or
extrapolation problem with optimistic maximization. It does not isolate a
single cause. Potential contributors include maximization bias across many
action components, undiscounted long-horizon bootstrapping, target lag, large
unscaled return magnitudes, near-constant gradient clipping, and applying the
learned policy on heuristic-generated probe states. No one factor was
independently manipulated in this experiment. Do not claim that EMC-Q alone
caused the failure, or that any one listed factor has been proven causal.

WHAT THE RESULTS MEAN
1. The optimization problem was not simply that the learner failed to learn:
   returns and deadline success improved substantially during training.
2. The value estimates became unreliable despite that policy improvement.
3. The failed Q screens affect all three architectures, so replacing EMC-Q with
   additive64/128 under the same training setup does not address this issue.
4. EMC-Q met the storage and local timing screens, but failed the predeclared
   policy-quality comparisons. Its proposed quality-preserving storage claim
   remains unsupported.

NEXT RESEARCH STEP
Do not resume training or alter the completed run. First freeze a new design
that addresses the demonstrated value-bound problem and distinguishes value
stability from policy quality. A mathematically justified nonpositive value
parameterization or a bounded finite-horizon return target could be tested,
but either changes the learner and must be compared under a new predeclared
protocol with disjoint seeds. Validate the value bound, episodic return,
deadline success, parameter storage, latency, and all comparators together.
Do not select a fix from the current fresh gate or present it as already
validated.
