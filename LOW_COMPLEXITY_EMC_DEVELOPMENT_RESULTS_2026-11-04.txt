VALUE CALIBRATION
All three architectures returned a positive greedy Q value on 100% of the

- EMC-Q: 57.75, 46.98, 65.32 for seeds2309,2333,2351 (limit10).

The shared calibration failure across all arms indicates that the frozen
training/reward setup did not yield well-calibrated return estimates. It does
not establish which training component caused the failure. The action-value
screen is diagnostic and cannot replace policy return and deadline outcomes.

COST SCREEN
EMC-Q retained its predeclared storage advantage versus additive128:
277,444 online parameter bytes, 65.7% of additive128's 422,016 bytes, meeting
the 70% cap. Its medians across the three fitted models were 0.2916, 0.2954,
and 0.2937ms; p95 values were 0.3487, 0.3496, and 0.3510ms. It met the local
5x median-ratio and 2.0ms p95 ceilings, while remaining slower than the
additive networks. This is machine-specific CPU timing only.

CONCLUSION
This run does not support the proposed claim that EMC-Q preserves the selected
return and deadline performance while reducing storage. The frozen screen is
failed even though the parameter-storage and local timing ceilings pass.
It does not establish low complexity, noninferiority, novelty, or deployment
readiness. The result is retained as a completed negative development result;
the protocol, thresholds, seeds, run records and checkpoints remain unchanged.

No follow-up model was trained from these outcomes. Before a new experiment,
diagnose the common value overestimation from saved learning and validation
records and decide whether the thesis claim or model objective must change.
Any follow-up must receive a new predeclared design and disjoint seeds. Do not
reuse this fresh gate to retune or select checkpoints.
