LOW COMPLEXITY ARCHITECTURE DESIGN
Working name: Single-Pass Interaction-Aware DDQN (SPI-DDQN)
Status: proposal for mechanics gate; not implemented or trained.

Problem to solve
The prior sequential candidate represented action dependence but required 32
stages and measured roughly124-125 times the additive selection latency. The
additive candidate was fast but cannot represent every joint-action interaction
exactly. SPI-DDQN tests whether a bounded interaction correction can retain
useful joint information with one shared encoder pass.

Architecture

Physical input: 533 values.
Shared encoder: 533 -> 96 -> 96, ReLU.
Parallel component logits: 96 -> 32 x 5.
Action embeddings: a learned 5 x 16 table shared by components.
Joint scorer input: encoded state, pooled selected-action embedding and a
low-rank interaction summary. Initial rank r=8.
Candidate decoding: take top2 choices per component, then beam-search complete
vectors with width8. The beam scorer is evaluated in batches.

The component heads generate a bounded candidate set. The joint scorer ranks
complete vectors within that set. The model does not claim exact optimization
over all5^32 joint actions. Beam coverage must be reported.

Why this may help
The shared encoder is computed once. Action interactions are handled by a
small global scorer rather than by32 sequential neural decisions. Batching the
beam candidates may keep the complete-slot path closer to the additive cost.

Risks

- top2 choices can exclude the physically useful action;
- beam width8 may still miss important combinations;
- the joint scorer adds parameters and candidate evaluations;
- off-policy DDQN targets for a bounded candidate set need explicit semantics;
- partial observation and hidden queue state remain unresolved;
- a lower parameter count may still fail to reduce measured latency.

Training semantics for a future pilot
Use one physical-slot transition and one complete joint action per decision.
The environment steps once per physical slot. The candidate action is the
beam-selected vector. A DDQN target uses the next-state candidate policy and
the same declared gamma=1 physical-slot objective. Do not use 32 staged
rewards. If the candidate-set maximization is not stable, stop and redesign
the learner rather than silently treating it as exact Q-learning.

Mechanics gate before training

1. Verify output shapes and legal action ranges.
2. Verify top-k and beam decoding on deterministic logits.
3. Verify the joint scorer changes when an interaction term changes.
4. Verify one complete-slot selection does one encoder pass and no environment
   step during timing.
5. Compare additive, sequential cached and SPI paths on common observations.
6. Check parameter bytes and complete-slot latency.
7. Require the candidate to beat the sequential path on latency before a
   learning smoke; if it does not, its low-complexity premise is weakened.

No performance, novelty or low-complexity claim is established by this design.
