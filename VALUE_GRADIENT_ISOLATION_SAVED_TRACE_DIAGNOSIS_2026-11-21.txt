VALUE-GRADIENT ISOLATION: SAVED-TRACE DIAGNOSIS
21 November 2026 (Asia/Shanghai)

FINDING AND DECISION
Two distinct problems are visible in the saved evidence. In initialization 2,
the primary sends too much simultaneous work to RSU 2, and recorded CPU
waiting explains many newly missed deadlines. Separately, the calibrated
value function drifts as its shared features change; blocking the V-to-hidden
TD gradient does not freeze those features or preserve calibration. Training
also samples a very different state/action distribution from greedy evaluation.

These are measured observations and algebraic/accounting results. They do
not identify one unique training-failure cause or prove that a particular new
controller will succeed. The declared outcome remains policy=false,
relative value=true, promising=false. No threshold, reward, seed, optimizer,
model or failed scientific decision was changed. No retraining was performed.

  P versus E: 358 / 22 / 9.

For 140 newly missed P-versus-E tasks, both controllers selected RSU 2.
Their mean additional P CPU queue wait is 137.144644 ms (median 139.373515;

equals the CPU queue-wait difference, verified per task.

Against A, 181 same-RSU2 newly missed tasks have 105.470269 ms mean additional

their counts together as independent failures.

The other 217 P-versus-E newly missed tasks changed destination: 110 moved
from E's local action to P's RSU2, and 107 moved from E's RSU1 to P's RSU2.
One further new miss kept RSU1. This accounts for all 358 new misses. No
claim is made that a single reassignment would independently remove them:
joint allocations determine which other tasks share each queue.

2. NO PERSISTENT EVALUATION QUEUE BACKLOG
All observed queue features are zero at decision boundaries for P/R/A/E/F,
in all three evaluation fits. All their evaluated tasks complete before the
next one-second slot. P's maximum task delays by fit are 324.102, 355.316
and 372.327 ms: above some task deadlines, below one slot.

P and E have bit-identical complete saved observations on every matched
evaluation decision in all fits, including UAV features. Thus their observed
action differences are not due to different decision-boundary states. P/R
also have identical evaluation observations in fit 2. A differs from P only
by tiny UAV battery values on 2.65625% of fit-2 observations; re-decoding
every core model on both populations gives identical actions there.

Do not describe this as missing observation of an existing persistent queue
or propose queue-feature additions solely on that explanation. The measured
deadline bottleneck is congestion formed AFTER simultaneous placement within
the slot. Current observed inputs already include each task's work/deadline;
whether a decoder learns to coordinate those allocations remains a separate
controller-design question. UAV state/battery can differ in other comparisons,
so this is not a claim that the complete environment is a contextual bandit.

4. SHARED FEATURES CONTINUE TO MOVE AFTER CALIBRATION
On the SAME 640 P evaluation states in each fit, compare final P with E's
unchanged calibrated handoff and fix the joint action to E's choice:

Fit   Mean delta Q(E action)   Mean delta V   Centered-branch contribution
0            -4.410651          -10.434183             +6.023531
1           -26.682032          -30.048852             +3.366820
2           -19.098421          -23.132183             +4.033763

The Q difference equals delta V plus the change in the centered branch
contribution, subject to recorded FP32 roundoff. This separates changes in
absolute values from the much smaller preferences between placement actions.

Let E's hidden features/readout be h0,w0,b0 and final P's be h1,w1,b1.
The symmetric algebraic split is:
  readout = 0.5*(h1+h0)*(w1-w0) + (b1-b0)
  features = 0.5*(h1-h0)*(w1+w0)
Their sum equals the explicit affine delta V. This averages the two possible
orders of changing features/readout; it is not a causal training intervention.

Fit    Readout contribution    Hidden-feature contribution   Sum
0             -2.345601                 -8.088585          -10.434185
1             +2.328504                -32.377358          -30.048854
2             +1.043022                -24.175207          -23.132184

Feature changes dominate P's negative value shift in fits 1/2; its changed
readout partly offsets them. The readout weight change norms are only 0.3081
and 0.5504, against final row norms 33.0643 and 40.4125. The affine attribution
is calculated in FP64 from saved FP32 features/weights. For these three P
evaluation-population decompositions, the largest FP64-versus-original-FP32
delta discrepancy is about 0.000144, far smaller than the measured shifts.
Scientific comparisons retain their original rules.

The mechanism blocks only V-to-hidden TD gradients. Branch TD and imitation
still update the shared hidden representation. Therefore the calibrated
readout no longer acts on its fitted feature map. Correct routing is already
verified in the completed first-update audit; it is not equivalent to fixed
features, stable Q values, or accurate values on a new policy.

The scalar common V cancels directly from branch argmin decisions. Large
value drift does not by itself prove the cause of RSU2 placement changes.
Branch changes, training dynamics and joint allocation must be distinguished.

5. IMITATION LOSS AND GREEDY SCORE GAINS DO NOT CERTIFY DEADLINES
On P's common evaluation states, its placement actions differ from E's in
20.1042%, 19.9427% and 31.0677% of vehicle decisions in fits 0/1/2. Movement
choices are unchanged. Mean P placement margins are only about 0.00127,
0.00108 and 0.00127. P's mean own-Q gain from its greedy joint action over
E's action is about 0.00293, 0.00271 and 0.00879; these are model scores,
not measured return gains from counterfactual simulations.

P's demonstration imitation loss decreases in each fit: 1.152412->1.071427,
1.151217->1.105380 and 1.153023->1.097497. Fit 2 still loses deadlines.
Average cross-entropy can improve while specific high-impact placement
mistakes remain. Do not use loss alone to replace the frozen policy screen.

Each behavior fraction uses all 2,000 saved online states; evaluation uses

replay draws, only about 35.4-35.9% of total sampled states have empty queues;
among online samples alone, only about 4.85-5.05%. The early congested behavior
is repeatedly sampled; these are weighted draws, not unique-state counts.
The last 200 updates still contain only about 25.0-25.8% empty-queue samples.

Replay terminal samples expose a substantial fitting error even though their
target bypasses all successor inference and equals the recorded reward:

Fit   Last-200 online terminal mean prediction / target
0                          +131.231091 / -27.273644
1                           +32.504340 / -25.868038

In those same windows, demonstration terminal mean predictions are -4.217871,
-34.157049 and -24.743221 against targets near -0.305. Errors have different
signs across populations. These are the actual logged predictions at update
time, not predictions reconstructed from unavailable intermediate weights.
They are not directly comparable to final greedy evaluation Q errors as if
all came from one common policy/state/action distribution.

Terminal bootstrap values are recorded and verified as zero. Consequently,
one cannot explain all this misfit solely by terminal successor bootstrapping.
The declared training schedule has not fitted even these direct terminal
targets well. DDQN is off-policy; coverage mismatch alone neither invalidates
the update nor proves a causal remedy. More training, a larger machine or a
new sampler is not demonstrated to fix it by these observations.

P mean preclip shared-hidden gradient norms are 0.1702/0.3991/0.3503 versus
A's 261.0494/149.1816/191.1843. Its total norm is much smaller, confirming a
substantial change in gradient behavior. Yet clipping remains 98.13%, 99.79%
and 100% of its updates. Its value-output gradient accounts on average for
48.58%, 61.70% and 65.76% of squared total norm. No historical per-loss full
gradient-direction replay is claimed: saved norms combine TD and retention.

WHAT IS ESTABLISHED, AND WHAT REMAINS OPEN
Established: source-correct selective routing occurred; many specific added
misses arise through recorded RSU2 CPU waiting; queues are empty before
evaluation decisions; shared-feature changes account algebraically for most
negative V shift; behavior/replay/evaluation coverage and terminal fitting
remain problematic; neither better imitation loss nor smaller gradients
guaranteed the required policy improvement.

Open: the unique causal chain from optimizer/replay/representation changes
to harmful branch preferences, and which prospective correction will improve
both return and deadlines across fresh fits at lower deployment cost. Saved
endpoints cannot reconstruct all intermediate training policies. No unchanged
failure is reclassified as a successful novelty or thesis contribution.
