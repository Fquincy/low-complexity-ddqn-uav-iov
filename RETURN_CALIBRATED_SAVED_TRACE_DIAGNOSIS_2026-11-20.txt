RETURN-CALIBRATED HANDOFF: SAVED-TRACE DIAGNOSIS
Date: 2026-11-20

DECISION
Offline diagnosis is complete. The frozen policy screen remains FAILED,
the value screen remains PASSED, and promising remains false. No training,
simulation, teacher inference or final evaluation was performed. No
historical model, reward, threshold, seed or optimizer setting was changed.

There are now two concrete findings to guide a prospective design decision:
1. Destination rankings change after DDQN continuation. The third fit sends
   more work to RSU 2, producing additional within-slot CPU queue waiting.
2. At the first saved update, the calibrated value readout dominates the
   TD gradient into the features shared with destination selection.
The second is an exactly reconstructed local training mechanism, not proof
that it caused every final miss or that a particular correction will pass.

Every A/B/C/D/E/F final checkpoint was evaluated on the same saved state

The changes therefore persist when holding the state fixed. A common V(s)
output shift alone cannot change the separable branch argmin. For A-E on
these fixed states, Q differences on the SAME E action decompose as:

Fit    Mean value-readout difference   Mean centered-branch difference
0                 -8.336186                         +2.108708
1                -10.520823                         +3.507783

This is the algebra Q(s,a)=V(s)-sum(centered branch costs), not causal
attribution of returns. Shared feature training can affect both terms.
F uses a nonlinear softplus transform; its Q-V quantity is not interpreted
as a linear centered branch contribution.

On the fixed demonstration population, A's imitation loss falls from
1.156216 to 1.091969, 1.150869 to 1.092099, and 1.184536 to 1.071869.
Nevertheless, 19.633333%, 17.233333% and 23.750000% of vehicle decisions
change from handoff. A lower average imitation loss does not imply preserving
each greedy action, meeting deadlines, or improving held-out return.

This directly accounts for their recorded delay difference. A task need

It does not establish the return of any unexecuted alternative allocation.

IMPORTANT OBSERVATION-BOUNDARY LIMIT
Across ALL saved A/E evaluation decision states, observed local workloads,
remote workloads, radio backlog and remote queue summaries are zero and
identical. Tasks/time are identical, and only small UAV-state differences
appear. All A/E/F tasks finish before the next one-second decision boundary;
deadline failures occur inside that slot, at shorter task deadlines.

Thus these traces do NOT test how the controllers react to an observed
nonzero backlog, and do not prove queue-feature blindness. No policy action
changes were found when swapping between paired saved A/E observations.
The relevant pressure is the simultaneous within-slot allocation of the
current tasks, before that slot's queues form. Current task descriptors
already exist in the observation; no claim that this information is absent
or that a new simulator/extra state is necessarily required is justified.

WHAT THE GRADIENT RECORDS AND RECONSTRUCTION ESTABLISH
Every A update is globally clipped. Mean shared-hidden fraction of squared
combined preclip gradient norm is 99.356%, 99.189% and 98.262% across fits.
B, without retention, also has very large shared-feature gradients. Combined
historical norms alone cannot determine separate TD/retention directions.

We therefore reconstructed ONLY the first saved update at the exact retained
E/A/B handoff in each fit, using saved replay and retention indices. A and
B have identical first TD batches. Predictions, detached targets, losses and
all logged preclip gradient-group norms reproduce. Fifteen offline autograd
evaluations separate TD, retention and the TD value/branch paths. No optimizer
was constructed or stepped; all parameter hashes remained unchanged.

Fit   Shared TD norm   Shared TD via V   Shared TD via branches   Shared keep
0        381.409272       381.409620             0.003677           0.038113
1        225.169483       225.169571             0.003017           0.036306
2        134.982343       134.981420             0.003916           0.046891

These are norms of gradient vectors before clipping, not additive scalar
contributions; vector sums and cross terms were checked. Initial V readout
weight norms are 44.383, 42.264 and 24.822. The entire calibrated branch-row
weight norms are about 0.039, 0.038 and 0.042. Retention has exactly zero
direct V-output gradient, while the TD value path strongly drives shared
features. The paths are differentiated at the same saved initial tensors.

This verifies an indirect route by which value fitting can disturb decision
features even though a common value offset cannot change argmin directly.
It does not prove that gradient routing caused every final ranking change.
These norms are NOT Adam parameter-step sizes: clipping and adaptive moments
prevent interpreting their ratios as proportional final weight changes.
No intermediate checkpoints exist to reconstruct that mechanism throughout
the entire training trajectory.

A's mean terminal Q-G errors remain -16.066, -10.310 and -11.242 across the
three fits, versus nonterminal means -9.589, -3.848 and -3.154. Passing the

curves or sufficient evidence of numerical divergence. Full distributions,
terminal masks, demo/online splits and first-gap decompositions are retained.

CONTROLS AND CLAIM LIMITS
A beats B/C/D/G on return and deadlines in all fits. Calibration shrinks
the first-update mean greedy-versus-sampled action gap from about
16.506/13.329/12.924 in C/D to 0.058/0.046/0.051 in A/B. Retention materially

do not overcome A's failed required comparisons against E and F.
The imitation-only arm is a control, not a replacement DDQN success claim.
The study has identical student parameter storage and no established
deployment speed improvement over F. Scientific/novelty claims do not change.

DIAGNOSTIC HARNESS CORRECTION
The first analysis attempt rejected batch-128 Q reproduction: FP32 batching
changed calibrated Q by up to 0.000240326 in the checked primary population.
At that state, single-state greedy/forward exactly reproduced the saved Q;
the checked actions matched. The corrected diagnosis uses the original
single-state evaluation boundary and separately reproduces original
batch-400 demonstration probes. Every demonstration single/batch action
comparison in the completed diagnosis agrees. The original diagnostic
script and failure receipt are preserved under:
validation/return_calibrated_diagnosis_harness_correction_2026-11-20_01/
No scientific tolerance, research evidence or original code was changed.

NEXT BOUNDED GATE
Prepare one prospective controller-design and comparison decision brief.
Prioritize the now-identified coupling between calibrated value fitting and
decision features; assess whether isolating that gradient path can preserve
useful decisions while retaining genuine DDQN policy improvement. The brief
must also account for within-slot joint allocation and the delay/deadline
tradeoff; simply changing a common Q offset or adding backlog inputs is not
an evidence-backed fix for these observed failures.

Check any proposed mechanism against related literature before calling it
novel. Specify an unmodified paired ablation, E, F, ordinary small DDQN and
the relevant stronger references/heuristics, complete storage/latency costs,
fresh development seeds and an untouched final set before any later run.
Do not presume success, relax historical criteria, select a best fit or
automatically retrain. If the mechanism cannot justify improvement over
imitation alone, record HOLD rather than treating imitation as a DDQN win.

Contribution one remains efficient DDQN offloading in UAV-IoV; security/trust
is deferred, and cloud/charging remain excluded. The frozen failed evidence,
