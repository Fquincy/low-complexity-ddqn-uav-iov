LOW COMPLEXITY PAIRED LEARNING SMOKE REVIEW
Date: 2026-11-01
Status: smoke complete; no full pilot promotion.

The SPI-DDQN candidate completed a paired 80-slot smoke against additive DDQN
for two model seeds. Both arms used the same workload seeds, 73 optimizer
updates, nine target copies and the same physical-slot budget per replicate.
The smoke logged exogenous pre-step workloads and beam candidate counts.

RESULTS (development smoke only)
Replicate | Arm | Slots | Updates | Mean slot reward | Deadline success
0 | additive | 80 | 73 | -18.498 | 26.72%
0 | SPI | 80 | 73 | -18.288 | 29.33%
1 | additive | 80 | 73 | -17.530 | 25.98%
1 | SPI | 80 | 73 | -18.659 | 22.13%

SPI was slightly better in replicate0 and worse in replicate1. The smoke is
far too small to establish noninferiority, training stability or a contribution.
The reported mean is an average of physical slot rewards, not a confirmatory
episode-level performance estimate. No heuristic or fresh final gate was run.
Beam candidate counts averaged4.24 and4.50 in the two replicates, below the
nominal maximum8 because duplicate candidates occur during top-k expansion.
This is an implementation detail, not evidence of adequate beam coverage.

DECISION
Do not launch the full low-complexity pilot yet. The candidate has passed the
mechanics and paired integration gates, but the learning smoke is mixed. Before
spending a larger budget, add a beam-coverage diagnostic against a wider
candidate reference and make the training/evaluation accounting episode-based.
The current SPI target uses a bounded candidate action set; its semantics and
coverage must be documented before a full DDQN comparison.

The smoke does not prove or disprove the low-complexity contribution. The
measured untrained cost gate remains: SPI4.917ms median, additive0.092ms,
sequential10.472ms. SPI is much faster than sequential but still much slower
than additive. Its parameter storage is lower, but quality is not established.
