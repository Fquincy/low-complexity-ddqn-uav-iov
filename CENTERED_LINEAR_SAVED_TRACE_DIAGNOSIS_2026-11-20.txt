CENTERED-LINEAR COMPARISON: SAVED-TRACE DIAGNOSIS
2026-11-20

FINDING
The BC-to-DDQN handoff begins with useful action preferences but values
that are already on the wrong return scale. The first DDQN update then
receives predominantly upward scalar pressure on nonterminal samples.
By the end, both the value head and branch contributions have grown on
fixed demonstration states, and the primary has changed many vehicle
destination rankings. Its realized policies shift work from local CPUs
to RSUs, where all its observed evaluation deadline misses occur.
This identifies an observed failure sequence and a concrete initialization
problem. It does not prove one mechanism explains every later failure.

1. THE SCALE PROBLEM EXISTS BEFORE DDQN
On each fit's same 400 demonstration states, primary greedy Q means:
Fit                    0           1           2
Before BC             0.498       0.300       0.288
After BC             43.229      33.359      39.155
After DDQN           89.406      72.141      91.555
These are fixed-state probes, not rollout returns. Under this experiment's
nonpositive reward definition, true finite-horizon remaining returns cannot
be positive. The supervised imitation objective gives no return target.
It trains branch preference logits; the value output has no direct imitation
loss term, although the shared hidden representation changes during BC.

For the centered model, Q = V + placement advantage + movement advantage,
where advantage is minus selected score plus the mean of all five scores.
BC makes selected actions distinct from alternatives, increasing the scalar
contributions without calibrating them to returns. Exact mean decomposition:
Fit/stage           V       Placement contribution   Movement contribution
0 BC               0.696          23.744                    18.789
0 final           28.343          26.487                    34.575
1 BC               0.902          16.992                    15.465
1 final           20.054          23.294                    28.792
2 BC              -4.625          24.372                    19.408
2 final           19.113          30.911                    41.531
Both value output and branch contributions drift; this is not just a
constant offset at the end. A state-only V offset cannot change greedy
actions directly, because every action in that state receives the same V.
The shared network can still couple value fitting and branch rankings.

For each nonterminal transition:
target - prediction = reward
  + [Q(current state, greedy) - Q(current state, sampled action)]
  + [Q(next state, greedy) - Q(current state, greedy)].
There is no target/online weight difference yet: the target is the BC copy.

0           -5.141             15.077               0.047             9.983
1           -5.286             10.650               0.129             5.493
2           -6.536             16.757              -0.223             9.998
Nonterminal samples: 63, 63 and 62 out of 64. The large learned action gap,

Mean clipped target-minus-prediction (Huber scalar pressure) is +1.000,

This is direct arithmetic evidence about initialization, not a proof that
all subsequent parameter updates increase Q or that exploration is a bug.

3. TERMINAL MASKING IS CORRECT; LATER TRAINING IS NOT MONOTONIC
Every inspected logged target equals reward + target_q within FP32
tolerance; terminal target_q is zero. No missing terminal-mask error found.
Terminal samples are 2.482%, 2.457%, 2.420% of replay draws in the primary.
Large terminal errors and unit-Huber saturation coexist with numerous
nonterminal samples. Their frequency alone does not establish that changing
sampling/weights would solve the problem.
Primary gradient norms exceed the frozen clip of 10 on 48.18%, 69.50% and
61.54% of updates. These are recorded preclip norms, not nonfinite failures.
Two-hundred-update summaries show initial upward pressure followed by mixed
or negative pressure. In fit 1, sampled-action prediction means eventually
become negative while greedy fixed-state values stay positive. Replay
prediction means must not be treated as a fixed-state Q trajectory.
Only before-BC, after-BC and final fixed-state probes/checkpoints exist;
we cannot date the exact first policy-ranking error or evaluate intermediate
policies without new experiments. The old control still has near-zero greedy
Q despite negative sampled-action replay predictions; neither is calibrated.
The uncentered-linear control also has badly inflated values yet much better
policy outcomes. Value inflation alone does not explain the primary's ranking
damage; the centering/control contrast matters and remains part of diagnosis.

4. THE DECISION FAILURE IS VEHICLE PLACEMENT AND RSU QUEUING
On the unchanged demonstration states, primary placement decisions changed
from BC to final on 58.13%, 70.63%, 65.66% of vehicle branches. Movement
decisions did not change. Teacher branch agreement fell from 50.48%, 48.85%,
51.39% to 42.01%, 38.27%, 40.74%. Branch agreement is not a policy return.
On primary evaluation states, comparing the final primary with BC-only
on EXACTLY THE SAME states changes 63.53%, 72.28%, 64.52% of placements;
movement differences are zero. The saved rollout actions were reproduced.

Each fit evaluates 19,200 tasks. Destination counts (local / RSU1 / RSU2):
Fit        BC-only                       Primary
0          10847 / 4415 / 3938            3820 / 7916 / 7464
1           9393 / 6571 / 3236             358 /10112 / 8730
2          10846 / 3646 / 4708            2718 /12457 / 4025
Neither of these policies sends evaluation tasks to UAV compute nodes.
All primary deadline misses are at the RSUs, not local execution or UAV
offloading: 630, 2,584, 4,307 misses, versus BC-only 10, 266, 20.
In fit 2, all 4,307 primary misses occur at RSU1. Its mean queue delay is
0.1800 seconds, before adding transmission and compute delay, under task
deadlines of 0.25-0.30 seconds. New primary-only misses versus BC-only:
622, 2,381, 4,292; BC-only misses avoided by primary: 2, 63, 5.
Primary misses appear throughout the episode, not only at termination.

The paired records have identical task IDs, sizes, cycles, arrivals and
deadlines. Their queues and physical states evolve under different actions.
Therefore destination/miss associations do not establish counterfactual
task-level causation or prove that any single replacement action is optimal.

CONTRIBUTION DECISION AND NEXT GATE
Keep both frozen scientific screens FAILED. Keep the earlier pilot's
promising result and its limitations; do not promote BC-only or another
secondary controller retrospectively as a validated new DDQN contribution.
The low-complexity focus remains; a measured DDQN improvement is unresolved.

The next bounded step should be ONE prospective brief addressing the
BC-to-DDQN handoff: put initial scalar values/action gaps on a defensible
return scale and explicitly test retention of the useful BC policy during
DDQN continuation. Specify the equations, role of fixed vs learned outputs,
mechanism ablation, BC-only and previous-baseline comparisons, calibration
and policy checks, costs, fresh seeds and stopping decision BEFORE training.
Any use of return calibration or imitation retention must be checked against
existing methods; those familiar tools are not automatically new novelty.
Do not patch the frozen model, add another architecture, tune from this run
or launch a new study in this diagnostic gate. A design brief is a hypothesis,
not a claim that this next attempt will work.

The final evaluation set and Word thesis remain unchanged. Security/trust
is deferred; cloud/charging are excluded from contribution-one experiments.
