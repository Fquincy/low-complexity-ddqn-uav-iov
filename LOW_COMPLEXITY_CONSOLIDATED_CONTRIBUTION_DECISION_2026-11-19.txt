1. WHERE WE ARE
We have implemented DDQN controllers, audited development experiments and
measured substantial parameter reductions. We do not yet have a controller
that satisfies its complete frozen scientific acceptance requirements.
An audit pass verifies saved evidence; it does not certify policy superiority.
Our topic remains efficient computational offloading in UAV-assisted IoV.
Parking a candidate does not abandon that topic or erase useful results.

2. EVIDENCE BY FAMILY, NOT BY EVERY CODE REVISION
This ledger covers the principal Low Complexity experimental families found
in the cited reviews. It is not a count of every historical Chapter 3 model,
engineering correction, ablation, smoke or timing attempt.

SPI / candidate-beam prototype: smoke and offline decoder checks, no full
research pilot. Much slower than additive DDQN. Early beam-coverage concerns
were subsequently qualified by the objective/gradient diagnosis; do not use
beam disagreement alone as proof that wider actions improve physical reward.

EMC-Q: completed 72,000-slot development comparison. Saved-artifact audit
passed; policy and value screens failed. Storage and the declared local cost
ceilings passed, but the primary remained slower than additive references.

Value-bound models: completed 120,000-slot comparison; audit passed. Of three
primary initializations, one passed every screen, one passed policy/calibration
but narrowly failed the median-ratio limit (5.009 versus 5.0), and one failed
all five policy comparisons. Aggregate acceptance failed. Preserve all three;
the passing initialization is not a confirmed successful method by itself.

Version 3: completed 440,000-slot comparison; audit passed. Storage and declared
cost screens passed. Deadline performance trailed compact64 by 12.2181 points
on the five-fit mean; strong heuristics and calibration comparisons failed.

Terminal anchor: completed 680,000-slot comparison with evaluation-only
continuation and audit. Cost and terminal-error checks passed; global value,
reference, heuristic and mechanism screens failed. Mean deadline deficits were
10.9854 points versus compact64 and 8.7236 versus wide128. Fixing the boundary
did not establish good whole-horizon policy/value behavior.

Deadline screen: completed 440,000-slot comparison and corrected full audit.
The declared screening mechanism passed: +11.297719 mean return and +24.055208
deadline percentage points versus its exact unmasked arm, both positive in
all five fits. Cost passed. Overall acceptance failed because stronger
same-width controls, some heuristic comparisons, price and calibration did
not meet their rules. This is useful measured component evidence, not proof
that generic feasibility masking is novel.

Horizon-normalized loss: completed 560,000-slot comparison; audit passed.
The correction reduced return and deadline success versus fresh paired
original-loss controls in every fit: means -7.753247 return and -13.984028
deadline points. Some mechanism and cost screens passed, but the proposed
correction and combined claim failed. Original-loss screened price24 controls
had deadline success 95.8247-99.6441%; those are exploratory control results,
not a replacement primary or final generalization evidence.

Assignment-dependent pricing (ADP): completed 600,000 training slots and
151,680 evaluation slots. Completion records AUDITED_COMPLETE. All seven
scientific screens failed. Primary beats plain small DDQN, compact/wide
references, nearest-RSU and UAV-selective on both outcomes in all five fits.
It loses to the stronger observed-load/radio heuristic in all five: mean
-2.5006 return and -2.2010 deadline points. Mechanism benefits are inconsistent.
Primary storage is 68,264 parameter bytes versus 194,944 compact64 and 422,016
wide128: 64.98% and 83.82% fewer bytes. It is larger than plain24 (67,364).
The matched median-cost screen fails; absolute p95 <=1ms passes. Three
eager/export action disagreements prevent exact deployment equivalence.

RQ24 and AR24: two subsequent synthetic engineering candidates, NOT completed
research-training failures. Correctness checks passed, matched median latency
failed. RQ24's one equivalent optimization still failed. AR24's fixed-path
profile did not establish sufficient removable overhead; its stop rule was
applied without another optimized implementation. Both remain parked. Neither
has measured trained-policy, deadline or calibration evidence.

3. WHAT WE CAN DEFEND NOW
Implemented compact DDQN offloading architectures and reproducible evaluation
in the named simulator; substantial online parameter-payload reduction versus
named compact/wide references; screening-related development policy gains;
terminal-value improvements; and clearly documented negative comparisons.
These are substantive implementation and experimental results worth retaining.
They do not yet establish the intended combined controller contribution.

Storage is not total deployment memory. Sub-millisecond latency is not speed
superiority. Earlier and later cost gates used different declared routes;
do not rank controllers by latency numbers from different sessions/backends.
Do not average unrelated seed studies to manufacture a confirmation result.
Large gains against a failed comparator fit do not establish robustness.

4. WHY ANOTHER AUTOMATIC REDESIGN IS NOT THE NEXT STEP
Different stages tested different hypotheses, rather than one universal
failure: approximation/training defects, value boundaries, deadline screening,
loss normalization, assignment interactions and complete runtime overhead.
Small networks can lose their speed advantage to physical feature/decoder
work. Good terminal values can coexist with poor policy or horizon-wide values.
Clipping and deadline-failure concentrations are diagnostic associations;
saved evidence does not establish a single causal optimizer explanation.
A faster computer cannot resolve the already observed policy/mechanism errors.

5. CLAIM-SCOPE DECISION
Original combined target: retain it as UNMET. Do not claim proven lightweight
or low-complexity performance preservation, reliable price/coupling superiority,
global value accuracy, optimal scheduling, deadline guarantees or UAV energy
savings. No final launch or Word-thesis claim revision is justified today.

Recommended route to assess prospectively: a storage-efficient DDQN controller
with an explicitly measured policy/latency tradeoff within the same offloading
problem. This is a proposed scope for review, not a successful result, topic
approval, automatic promotion of AR24 or permission to weaken old criteria.
Only use the wording if its scientific rationale and fresh evidence support it.
The stronger heuristic must remain in a future comparison; specify whether
the claim is dominance or an explicit tradeoff and why that serves deployment.
Keeping the original faster-than-wide target instead requires a materially
justified new mechanism/runtime idea before spending another training budget.

Value interpretation must be resolved explicitly. Realized own-policy Q-G
error is not exact conditional expected Q or Q-star error. Useful action
ranking need not imply accurate scalar calibration. Nevertheless calibration
was mandatory in the completed protocols: those decisions remain FAILED.
A future role for calibration must be scientifically justified before fresh
outcomes, with finite/sign/terminal/scale diagnostics still reported. Do not
reclassify an endpoint merely because it failed.

Novelty is separate from all these metrics. Branching DDQN, action masks,
congestion awareness and coordinate/potential methods have prior literature.
Existing targeted literature notes do not establish full priority clearance.
An exact implemented distinction and an informative matched ablation remain
necessary. Thesis sufficiency and approved-scope acceptance cannot be promised.

6. NEXT BOUNDED STEP: ONE PROSPECTIVE CLAIM/VALIDATION RATIONALE
Write a design-neutral rationale before selecting a successor or new seeds:
(a) State the offloading deployment need and primary claim: original joint
    dominance target versus explicitly justified storage/policy/cost tradeoff.
(b) Define complete storage and action-latency boundaries, acceptable policy
    margins, initialization uncertainty and calibration interpretation from
    scientific/deployment requirements, not by choosing limits that pass old fits.
(c) Map the precise proposed improvement against closest full papers; state
    the indispensable same-width/no-mechanism control and equally informed
    observed-load/radio comparator. No generic masking novelty claim.
(d) Specify GO only if a defensible distinction, affordable runtime path and
    meaningful fresh test can all be stated. Otherwise HOLD; do not start a
    width sweep or add another physical surrogate by default.
This deliverable is not a frozen research protocol or authorization to train.
Only after it passes review should one candidate be selected, a fresh paired
development protocol frozen and a separate explicit launch decision made.
Previously consumed data can motivate that plan but cannot confirm it.
