1. WHY NEAR-ZERO Q AND USEFUL ACTION RANKING COEXIST
The plain compact student computes branch placement/motion scores and chooses
independent allowed minima. For the selected action let
  C=bias+sum_i placement_i,a_i/30+sum_j motion_j,a_j/2,
  Q=-softplus(C),  dQ/dC=-sigmoid(C).
Branchwise classification pushes teacher choices below alternatives. It does
not directly constrain the scalar bias or the cost scale/offset to represent
long-term return. Adding a state-dependent common offset to every choice in
one branch leaves its softmax probabilities and argmin unchanged but changes
C and Q,unless compensated elsewhere. Thus good classification alone cannot
identify a calibrated scalar critic. This is established mathematics,not a
novel mechanism or an experimentally proven sole cause of every failure.

Mean replay predictions become more negative through training,and recorded

11.30,13.71,11.42;clipping affects many updates. Replay includes non-greedy
actions with different scalar magnitudes;shared weights can change preferences
through their gradients even when greedy-action Q is saturated.
All400saved demo-state joint actions differ between BC-end and final policy
in each fit. Branch agreement falls from BC-end to final,but final closed-loop
policy still beats the paired control. These observations establish policy
drift during DDQN,not beneficial causal improvement from the DDQN stage.
The frozen pilot did NOT evaluate BC-only closed-loop behavior,so that missing
comparison cannot be reconstructed by evaluating final weights on old states.

Mean signed logged TD residuals decrease across update quarters,but samples,
policies and target critics change. This is not proof of convergence or good
conditional expected values. Own-policy RMSE/calibration remains poor. Do not
equate strong terminal residuals or lower loss with valid full-horizon values.
